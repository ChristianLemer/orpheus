Halcyon Ferris

34 keys · split 3×5+2 · vial · tapping term 175 ms

Thirty-four keys, and every one of them a letter. Everything else — digits, symbols, arrows, the mouse — lives underneath, and a thumb decides which world you are in. Press one of the four thumb keys below to go there. Nothing else on this page changes the layer, because nothing else changes it on the board either.

Same finger, same modifier

Flip Show holds and the home row stops being letters. A becomes Shift, S becomes Ctrl, D Alt, F Gui — and the right hand mirrors it exactly.

Shift is always a pinky. Ctrl is always a ring finger. Which hand you use depends only on which one is free, and the hand holding the modifier is never the hand pressing the letter. The mapping survives every layer: Bksp sits on the middle finger in Nav, so holding it gives Alt+Backspace — delete word — for free.

The thumbs carry the design

The thumb is the strongest digit and the least busy: it holds no letters. So it holds the layers instead — one thumb, one world, no chords to memorise.

Two of the four also emit a character when tapped rather than held. Space is the most frequent key on the board, so it takes the best thumb position and pays rent by opening the most-used layer as well.

What it costs

Roughly three weeks of stray modifiers. The tapping term — 175 ms here — is the whole trade: too short and fast typing throws modifiers, too long and deliberate holds feel stuck. No value is right for everyone.

And combos fight home-row mods. Both start a timer on keydown, so a combo across two adjacent home-row keys races itself: F+D yields Gui+D, a bare d, or nothing. Combos belong on the top and bottom rows.
